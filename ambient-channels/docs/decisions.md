## Risk R1 — "No AI" is a selling point for part of this audience
Midnight Blue Hollow, the strongest Christmas competitor (241K views in 7 days), titles its
videos "4K No AI" and says in every description that its art is hand-drawn and its music
human-made "because of how much AI-generated content has taken over these spaces".
Implications for us:
1. We never claim "No AI". Descriptions state AI-assisted honestly (YouTube disclosure rules too).
2. Craft must not look AI-generic: no plastic sheen, no melted details, consistent light — G1 is strict.
3. Worship: lean on our own locally rendered piano arrangements (no AI music at all in W1–W3),
   which is a real, honest differentiator ("original piano arrangements").
4. Watch comments on pilots for anti-AI reactions; if they dominate, G7 decides whether a
   channel needs more human-made elements (owner-drawn touches, licensed human music).

## Risk R2 — Content ID false matches on public-domain hymns
See channels/worship/CHANNEL.md. Hymns ≤ 30% of a video (≤ 50% for Christmas), free
arrangements, keep MIDI/project files as proof for disputes.

## Risk R3 — Mass-production signals (inauthentic content policy, July 2026 clarification)
Pacing ≤ 1 upload / 2 days per channel during pilots, every scene unique, no template titles
reused verbatim, real variation in music sets.

## 2026-11-17 — Palette exception for public-domain carols
Public-domain carols and hymns keep the mode they are printed in; melody notes are never changed to fit a
key. Their tonic is chosen from the channel palette. So F1's Wexford Carol is G mixolydian and W3's
O Come, O Come, Emmanuel is D minor (aeolian). Originals stay strictly inside the palette.
Why: changing a printed note to fit a palette would break the melody-accuracy gate (two-transcription
cross-check) and make the carol sound wrong to listeners who know it. Raised by the G0 reviewer (W3 round 5, F1 fix check).

## 2026-11-17 — Direction after the owner's YouTube browsing (owner asked the orchestrator to decide)
Seen in the feed: cozy Christmas rooms (fastest growing: brand-new channels at 30–50K views/day) and famous-fantasy
places (Moria, the Shire, Narnia).
Decision:
1. **Page & Lantern = cozy rooms inside fantasy worlds.** Through Christmas: every video is a warm room or snug place
   in a fantasy world, with a big lit tree, a fireplace, a snowy window, one sleeping animal, soft harp/piano. This
   combines both trends and keeps the channel's identity. F1 (elven inn + fox) gets a big lit Christmas tree.
2. **Famous-world feel without famous names.** Dwarven halls, a hill-burrow village, a lamp-post in a snowy wood, an
   elven valley: the places people search for, but our own art, our own names, our own music. No "LOTR", "Moria",
   "Shire", "Hobbit", "Narnia" in titles, tags or pictures: those names and their music belong to their owners
   (copyright and trademark), and one claim can cost a whole channel's revenue. After Christmas these epic places
   become the main series.
3. **Prayer Pastures stays as planned** (W3 O Holy Night in the shepherds' field).
4. **No new channel now.** Two channels done well beats three done thin; revisit in January with real data.
5. Music for all of this is soft and dark (as measured on the leaders), made locally.
